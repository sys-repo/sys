operation-plus-release errors and successful-body release failure without spurious rollback.

The three previously unproved branches now have ten deterministic cases in the new
`-test/-manifest.failure.test.ts`, under that same staging owner:

- Failed copy: both thrown and returned errors, each leaving either partial regular-file bytes or
  symlink residue. Regular bytes acquire an exact frozen checksum record and can be retracted;
  failed retention preserves the original error plus the exact retention error, with no ledger entry
  or deletion of the symlink/foreign target. Two further controls cover no manifest residue and
  ordinary payload residue without inventing manifest-deletion authority.
- Returned failed manifest write: partial regular bytes are retained by their actual checksum and
  removed through the real ledger; unsafe residue produces the exact ordered aggregate, original
  cause, and no deletion of unowned bytes. Both cases preserve the foreign file.
- Successful body followed by temporary cleanup failure: actual metadata-only replacement causes the
  real document fence to refuse deletion. The unchanged-root control retracts the root; the
  replaced-root case retains both original records and replacements, and preserves both errors in
  order with the exact aggregate message and original cause. This is not A04's body-failure branch
  and does not equate a content pin with deletion authority.

Only narrow internal dependencies were added: a defaulted `Fs.copyFile` parameter, and defaulted
write/removal functions in finalization. Defaults, public Deploy configuration, real identity and
ledger checks, and existing tests remain intact. Dependencies are explicitly threaded rather than
spreading caller arguments. No global monkey-patching, filesystem fault framework, permission
expansion, or reduced assertions. This is added branch proof, not a discovered product defect or an
old-code regression-red claim. Independent Tools review must still assess proof adequacy.
